> # Twinfang
> ==🟠Uncommon== ==🔴Conjoined==
> ---
> **Price** 12 gp; **Bulk** 1
> **Category** Advanced
>> A pair of slender, curved swords whose pommels twist and lock together. Its runes are etched along the locking collar both hilts share, so their magic flows into whichever blade strikes.
> ---
> **Joined** 1d8 S; **Hands** 2; **Group** sword
> **Traits** finesse, parry, sweep
>
> **Split** (each fang) 1d6 S; **Hands** 1; **Group** sword
> **Traits** agile, finesse, twin
>
> ---
> #### Traits
> **Conjoined** Conjoined weapons have two forms similar to Combination weapons. Switching between forms requires an Interaction action. However, if your last action was a successful Strike against a foe with this weapon, you can Interact to change the weapon's form as a free action and Strike with that form. 
> Since a Conjoined weapon is is one weapon with multiple forms, both forms share any fundamental rules. You can put a property rune on a Conjoined weapon as long as it is appropriate for either of the two forms, but if only one of the forms meets the property rune's requirements, the effects of the property rune only apply for that form.
> A single Interact action draws or stows the whole weapon in either form. Drawing it split requires both hands to be free.
> **Uncommon** Something of uncommon rarity requires special training or comes from a particular culture or part of the world. Some character choices give access to uncommon options, and the GM can choose to allow access for anyone. Less is known about uncommon creatures than common creatures. They typically can't be summoned. The DC of [Recall Knowledge](https://2e.aonprd.com/Actions.aspx?ID=26) checks related to these creature is increased by 2.
> **Finesse** You can use your Dexterity modifier instead of your Strength modifier on attack rolls using this melee weapon. You still calculate damage using Strength.
> **Parry** This weapon can be used defensively to block attacks. While wielding this weapon, if your proficiency with it is trained or better, you can spend a single action to position your weapon defensively, gaining a +1 circumstance bonus to AC until the start of your next turn.
> **Sweep** This weapon makes wide swinging attacks. When you attack with this weapon, you gain a +1 circumstance bonus to your attack roll if you already attempted to attack a different target this turn using this weapon.
> **Agile** The multiple attack penalty you take with this weapon on the second attack on your turn is –4 instead of –5, and –8 instead of –10 on the third and subsequent attacks in the turn.
> **Twin** These weapons are used as a pair. When you attack with a twin weapon, you add a circumstance bonus to the damage roll equal to the weapon’s number of damage dice if you have previously attacked with a different weapon of the same type this turn. The weapons must be of the same type, but they don’t need to have the same runes.

## Feats

### Viper Dedication
**FEAT 2** · Archetype · Dedication
**Prerequisites** trained in martial weapons

You've drilled with the twinfang until changing its shape feels as natural as breathing. You gain access to the twinfang, and for the purpose of determining your proficiency, the twinfang is a martial weapon for you.

**Slither** When you Step while holding both halves of your twinfang or the joined twinfang, you can switch its form as part of that Step.

**Special** You can't select another dedication feat until you've gained two other feats from the Viper archetype.

### Coiled Stance ◆
**FEAT 4** · Archetype · Stance
**Prerequisites** Viper Dedication
**Requirements** You're wielding the twinfang in its joined form.

You grip the twinfang at its center, ready to cut with either end. While you're in this stance, the twinfang's trailing blade counts as a second melee weapon held in your other hand. It's a 1d6 slashing weapon in the sword group with the agile and finesse traits, and it shares the twinfang's runes.

You can use abilities that require you to wield two melee weapons, each in a different hand, using the joined twinfang and its trailing blade. You can't use abilities that require a two-handed weapon while you're in this stance. The stance ends if you split the twinfang.

### Serpent's Turn ◆◆
**FEAT 4** · Archetype · Flourish
**Prerequisites** Viper Dedication
**Requirements** You're holding both halves of your twinfang or the joined twinfang.

You cut with the full blade, then wrench it apart for a second strike before your foe can recover. If the twinfang is split, you join it as part of this activity. Strike a foe with the joined twinfang, then split the twinfang and Strike the same foe with one fang. Both Strikes use your current multiple attack penalty.

If both Strikes hit, combine their damage, and then add any other applicable effects from both Strikes. You add any precision damage only once, to the Strike of your choice. Apply resistances and weaknesses to the combined damage only once. Serpent's Turn counts as two attacks when calculating your multiple attack penalty.

### Writhing Arc ◆◆
**FEAT 6** · Archetype · Flourish
**Prerequisites** Viper Dedication
**Requirements** You're holding both halves of your twinfang or the joined twinfang.

You whip the twinfang through a wide, serpentine arc. If the twinfang is split, you join it as part of this activity. Make a melee Strike with the joined twinfang and compare the attack roll result to the ACs of up to two foes, each of whom must be within your reach and adjacent to the other. Roll damage once and apply it to each creature you hit.

The twinfang's sweep trait applies its bonus to this Strike even if you haven't attacked another target this turn. Writhing Arc counts as two attacks for your multiple attack penalty.

### Shed Skin ⟳
**FEAT 8** · Archetype
**Prerequisites** Viper Dedication
**Trigger** A creature you can see targets you with a melee attack.
**Requirements** You're holding both halves of your twinfang or the joined twinfang.

You twist away from the blow, reshaping your weapon as you move. Switch the twinfang's form, and you gain a +2 circumstance bonus to AC against the triggering attack. If the attack misses you, you can Step as part of this reaction.

### Uncoiled Fury ◆◆◆
**FEAT 10** · Archetype
**Prerequisites** Viper Dedication
**Frequency** once per minute
**Requirements** You're holding both halves of your twinfang or the joined twinfang.

You unleash the twinfang's full rhythm in one breathless flurry. If the twinfang is joined, split it. Strike twice, once with each fang. Then join the twinfang and Strike twice more with it. Each Strike can target any creature within your reach, and switching forms during this activity doesn't take any additional actions.

Each attack counts toward your multiple attack penalty, but don't increase your penalty until you've made both attacks in a pair.

## Playing a Viper

- **Fighters** are already trained in advanced weapons, but the dedication moves the twinfang onto their martial proficiency, which puts it at expert right away. Double Slice and Twin Parry cover the split form, and Swipe and Power Attack cover the joined form. Serpent's Turn is the archetype's version of Double Slice that uses both forms in one activity.
- **Rogues** get sneak attack from either form, since both are finesse. A thief rogue adds Dexterity to damage in both forms too.
- **Rangers** can use Twin Takedown with the fangs, or with the joined form while in Coiled Stance.
- **Swashbucklers** get finesse finishers in both forms, and the joined form's parry pairs well with Twin Parry.
