> #### Traits
> **Conjoined:** Conjoined weapons have two forms similar to Combination weapons. Switching between forms requires an Interaction action. However, if your last action was a successful Strike against a foe with this weapon, you can Interact to change the weapon's form as a free action and Strike with that form. 
> Since a Conjoined weapon is is one weapon with multiple forms, both forms share any fundamental rules. You can put a property rune on a Conjoined weapon as long as it is appropriate for either of the two forms, but if only one of the forms meets the property rune's requirements, the effects of the property rune only apply for that form.
> A single Interact action draws or stows the whole weapon in either form. Drawing it split requires both hands to be free.
> **Uncommon:** Something of uncommon rarity requires special training or comes from a particular culture or part of the world. Some character choices give access to uncommon options, and the GM can choose to allow access for anyone. Less is known about uncommon creatures than common creatures. They typically can't be summoned. The DC of [Recall Knowledge](https://2e.aonprd.com/Actions.aspx?ID=26) checks related to these creature is increased by 2.
> **Finesse:** You can use your Dexterity modifier instead of your Strength modifier on attack rolls using this melee weapon. You still calculate damage using Strength.
> **Parry:** This weapon can be used defensively to block attacks. While wielding this weapon, if your proficiency with it is trained or better, you can spend a single action to position your weapon defensively, gaining a +1 circumstance bonus to AC until the start of your next turn.
> **Sweep:** This weapon makes wide swinging attacks. When you attack with this weapon, you gain a +1 circumstance bonus to your attack roll if you already attempted to attack a different target this turn using this weapon.
> **Agile:** The multiple attack penalty you take with this weapon on the second attack on your turn is –4 instead of –5, and –8 instead of –10 on the third and subsequent attacks in the turn.
> **Twin:** These weapons are used as a pair. When you attack with a twin weapon, you add a circumstance bonus to the damage roll equal to the weapon’s number of damage dice if you have previously attacked with a different weapon of the same type this turn. The weapons must be of the same type, but they don’t need to have the same runes.

> **Archetype** Viper
> You are a hunter of mighty beasts. You've studied one of nature's deadliest predators─one capable of overcoming foes greater even than themselves: the viper. Swift and Agile, with dual blades like fangs to bite or combine them into a single weapon to weave about the battlefield.
> You gain access to the Twinfang weapon. For the purpose of proficiency, you treat the Twinfang weapon as a martial weapon.
> You gain the Twinfang Bite feat.
> #### Traits
> **Archetype:** This feat belongs to an archetype.
> **Dedication:** Each archetype’s dedication feat represents your character’s dedicated effort learning a new set of abilities, making it impossible to split your focus and pursue another archetype at the same time. Once you take a dedication feat, you can’t select a different dedication feat until you complete your dedication by taking two other feats from your current archetype. You can’t retrain a dedication feat as long as you have any other feats from that archetype.

Combine the damage from both Strikes and apply resistances and weaknesses only once. This counts as two attacks when calculating your multiple attack penalty.
